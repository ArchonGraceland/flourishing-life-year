How to read this map

Each county's composite is the geometric mean of its available domain percentile ranks (purchasing power, wealth, family, health, education). Counties with fewer than 4 of 5 domains usable are gray — the index does not impute or extrapolate.

The geometric-mean rule is deliberate: it rewards counties that are broadly doing well across all five dimensions and penalizes lopsided performance. A county with the country's highest credentials but low skilled-trade share, low homeownership, or atypical family structure will not appear in the top despite a strong showing on any single dimension. The methodology page explains why.

All scores are reproducible from the federal sources listed on the methodology page, fixed for this release. If you can't reproduce a number, it's a bug.

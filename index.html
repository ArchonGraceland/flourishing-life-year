§ 01 — The Unit

A single number for the measurable part of a good life.

A Flourishing Life Year is the amount of human flourishing produced in one year, benchmarked against a national reference. It's the unit we use to describe places and people with the same math — the same federal data, the same five domains, the same arithmetic.

The idea borrows from the Quality-Adjusted Life Year used in health economics, and generalizes it. A year in which earnings, savings, family, health, and education all meet a national reference counts as one Flourishing Life Year. A year that falls short is discounted. A year that exceeds the reference scores above 1.0.

Applied to a county, it produces a map of how well places sustain flourishing lives. Applied to a person, it produces a Flourishing Age — where your life stands, measured against the national age trajectory, across the same five domains. Same unit. Same data. Same honesty.

The same arithmetic applies to a person or a place. Each domain score is a national percentile rank — where this entity stands relative to all U.S. counties (or all Americans) on that domain, scaled to [0.01, 1.00]. The composite is the geometric mean across the five (the fifth root of the product) — a deficit in any one domain pulls the whole result down, because flourishing isn't additive.

§ 03 — The Framework

Five domains. One year.

The same five domains measure a place and measure you. For each, we build a national age-trajectory curve from federal microdata, then see where your inputs — and your county's indicators — land on it. The assessment gives you a Flourishing Age. These are the benchmarks it's measured against.

01 / PURCHASING POWER

Does a paycheck go far here?

Real purchasing power: median household income deflated by the local cost of living. BEA publishes Regional Price Parities at state and metro level — not at county level — so each county gets its metro RPP if it sits inside a metro area, or its state's overall RPP otherwise. The imputation rule is documented, including the known limitation that the state-overall figure is metro-weighted. A dollar in Mississippi and a dollar in San Francisco buy different lives; this domain measures what the dollar actually does.

02 / WEALTH ACCUMULATION

Can people build something that lasts?

A proxy stack for household balance-sheet position: homeownership rate, median home value, and the share of county adjusted gross income that comes from dividends and capital gains. There is no federal wealth survey at county level — these three together form the most defensible substitute.

How long do people live, and how well?

Life expectancy at birth from the NCHS Small-area Life Expectancy Estimates Project (USALEEP), with age-adjusted mortality from CDC WONDER as the suppression-friendly fallback for small counties. The most legible single measure of how long lives last here.

05 / USEFUL EDUCATION

Does schooling lead somewhere?

Education here means human capital, not academic credentialism. Two components, multiplied: the share of adults with a post-secondary credential (associate's or bachelor's-and-above) and the share employed in skilled trades — construction, installation, maintenance, repair. The master electrician and the BA count equally.

§ 04 — How It Works

Built openly, from the ground up.

Every indicator is sourced from a public federal dataset, transformed in the open, and scored transparently. If a county-level number doesn't exist honestly, we don't fabricate one. Some gaps stay gaps — and we say so.

i.

Source

Pull from the American Community Survey (Census), the Bureau of Economic Analysis (BEA), IRS Statistics of Income (SOI), and CDC's National Center for Health Statistics. No proprietary data, no private licenses, no black-box APIs.

ii.

Harmonize

Crosswalk tract and county geographies to a consistent unit (50 states + DC). Where data is too unreliable to publish, suppress and label. Never impute, model, or fill from neighbors.

iii.

Score

Convert each indicator to a national percentile rank (1–100). Compose with equal weights — within each domain and across the five — using a geometric mean. A deficit in any one domain visibly pulls the composite down.

iv.

Publish

Release data, code, and methodology together. Versioned annually (vYYYY.MM, anchored to the ACS December release). Each prior version preserved and recomputable from the same inputs.

A county's composite requires data on at least 4 of 5 domains. With one domain suppressed, the composite is computed from the four available and flagged on hover. With two or more suppressed, the county shows as gray on the map. No imputation, ever — no state averages, no nearest-neighbor fill, no modeled estimates. The number is either reproducible from federal data, or it isn't.

Two clarifications. (1) Suppression vs reliability. CDC WONDER itself suppresses death counts of 9 or fewer; our additional n < 20 rule is a reliability threshold applied on top, not a contradiction of WONDER's own publication policy. (2) The county-RPP imputation in the purchasing-power domain is our own deterministic rule (metro RPP for metro-attached counties; the state's overall RPP otherwise) — it is not a BEA product. BEA does not publish a per-state non-metropolitan RPP, only a single national non-metro figure, so the state-overall figure is the most local available signal for non-metro counties even though it is metro-weighted. Documented because it is the one place this methodology relaxes the no-imputation rule, and only because BEA does not publish below the metro level.

This product uses the Census Bureau Data API but is not endorsed or certified by the Census Bureau.

Directional honesty over false precision

A rank-order that's right is worth more than a decimal score that's wrong. Where the data can't support county-level precision, we aggregate up rather than invent.

Indicators, not index worship

The composite matters less than its components. Every page shows the underlying indicators driving the score — drill down until the claim is falsifiable.

Open by construction

Methodology, source list, transformation code, and raw outputs are all public. If you can't reproduce our number, it's a bug.
